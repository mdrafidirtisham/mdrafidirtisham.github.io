01 // Overview

The Problem Situation

The Rohingya are a Muslim ethnic minority who have faced decades of persecution in Myanmar, where they are denied citizenship and restricted in movement, healthcare and education. A military crackdown in 2017 drove a mass exodus into Bangladesh, the main destination because of its early recognition of humanitarian need, its proximity and a shared religion (Milton et al., 2017).

Today more than a million refugees live in overcrowded camps. Bangladesh is struggling to support the influx, and host communities feel the strain: local people report losing access to their own resources and being undercut by refugees willing to work for lower wages (Rahman, 2010).

The engagement used SSM to structure the problem, drew on stakeholder perspectives to surface current issues and ideal worldviews, and ended with recommendations that address the pain points of each key party.

▸ Why a soft-systems approach

Cause and effect are deeply uncertain. Refugees need safety and rights, Bangladesh and its local communities worry about resources and security, and Myanmar resists responsibility. A workable answer has to hold all of these worldviews at once, which is exactly what SSM is built for.

06 // Conflicting Pairs & PQR SSM STAGE 3

Two Conflicts Drive the Situation

The four key stakeholders form two conflicting pairs. Each was given a relevant system, first stated as a PQR: do P (what), by Q (how), in order to achieve R (why).

The refugees depend on the support the Bangladeshi government provides, and that dependency defines the relationship. The government holds considerable power over how the crisis is handled and how its effects on local resources and residents are managed, so the refugees' needs and rights are not always at the top of its priorities (BBC News, 2021).

The international community has significant influence through relief and support for refugees and through sanctions and other pressure on Myanmar. Myanmar holds decision-making authority and territorial control but engages little, and has been widely criticised for its treatment of the Rohingya (Human Rights Watch, 2017).

07 // CATWOE, Root Definitions & 3 E's SSM STAGE 3

Four Systems, Four Worldviews

CATWOE (Customers, Actors, Transformation, Worldview, Owners, Environmental constraints) makes sure every relevant party and constraint is considered (Jackson, 2003). Each analysis builds into a root definition: the purpose, stakeholders and boundary of the system (Checkland and Poulter, 2006). The 3 E's set how each system would be judged.

08 // Conceptual Models SSM STAGE 4

What Each System Would Have to Do

Each root definition becomes an activity model: an input is transformed through a sequence of activities into an output, monitored against standards and controls. The models are the yardstick for comparing ideal with reality (Jackson, 1982).

09 // Complementary Analysis

PESTEL: The Client's External Environment

To go beyond SSM, a PESTEL analysis (Johnson, Scholes and Whittington, 2008) examined the macro-environment the Bangladeshi government operates in: the forces that will make any recommended change easier or harder.

PPolitical
  • The administration is under pressure to control and end the situation.
  • Bangladesh has collaborated with the international community, but Myanmar has put political obstacles in the way of refugees' return.
  • As a democracy, Bangladesh faces scrutiny over human rights and refugee protection.
EEconomic
  • Meeting refugees' basic needs (food, shelter, healthcare) burdens national infrastructure and the economy.
  • The crisis has hit tourism and public perception, with possible effects on growth and foreign investment.
SSocial
  • The influx and demand on resources have had a huge social impact, raising the risk of tension and violence.
  • Pressure is greatest in the local communities hosting the refugees.
TTechnological
  • The government has relied mostly on conventional methods to manage the crisis.
  • Technology could help track and manage the influx, deliver healthcare and education, and improve coordination with the global community.
EEnvironmental
  • Hills and forests are cleared and trees cut down to build temporary camps.
  • Settlement and infrastructure put short- and long-term stress on resources and the ecosystem.
LLegal
  • The crisis has highlighted concerns over refugee protection and Rohingya human rights.
  • The international community has put legal pressure on the government to protect refugees.
  • Refugee management laws and procedures need reform.

10 // Recommendations SSM STAGES 5–6

From Ideal Models to Feasible Change

Each conceptual model was compared with the real-world situation. Where the gap was large enough, a specific change was proposed and placed on an ease–benefit matrix so the client can see which changes are quick wins and which are major projects.

11 // Conclusion

No Resolution Without Every Party

The analysis concludes that the crisis needs cooperation from all stakeholders, and points to five things that have to happen together.

  1. 01Support

    Keep supporting the refugees in the camps.

  2. 02Balance

    Balance this with the demands of the Bangladeshi government and its citizens.

  3. 03Recognise

    Press the Myanmar government to recognise Rohingya citizenship and rights.

  4. 04Repatriate

    Work towards returning refugees safely to their homeland.

  5. 05Pressure

    Keep up pressure from the international community.
